🔍 Top Intelligence Findings from 205 MACH Entries

— Academic governance dominates: 95 of 205 entries (46%) are academically maintained — reflecting the research-first origins of open-source healthcare tooling. Only 23 are vendor-led.
— Oncology is the most represented clinical domain with 66 entries — driven by digital pathology, imaging, and genomics tooling concentrated in cancer research.
— Apache-2.0 is the dominant licence (62 entries, 30%) — the enterprise-safe choice. MIT follows at 39. Only 7 entries use copyleft (GPL-3.0), reducing commercial adoption friction.
— Python is overwhelmingly the language of healthcare AI: 66 of 84 software/model entries with a declared language use Python (79%). Java is a distant second at 12, reflecting legacy EHR/FHIR tooling.
— Production maturity is the norm: 158 of 205 entries (77%) are rated production — MACH deliberately excludes prototype-only tools, so this reflects curation quality, not field-wide maturity.
— Digital pathology & medical imaging are overrepresented in keywords — "digital-pathology" (32), "medical-imaging" (31), "dicom" (27) — reflecting strong open-source tooling in imaging vs. gaps in clinical workflow and EHR software.
— FHIR adoption is real but not dominant: 21 entries carry the "fhir" keyword. Interoperability is still a gap — 13 entries tagged "interoperability" out of 205.
— Self-hosted deployment dominates (165 entries): Healthcare data sovereignty concerns drive on-premise preference. Only 36 entries are cloud-only — consistent with NHS, EU, and hospital IT policies.
