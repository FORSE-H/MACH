The open-source healthcare landscape

Healthcare does do open source, and you can see it here.

Proven open tools for research and care already exist, scattered across repositories and institutional websites. MACH helps you find them, trust them, and reuse and support them instead of rebuilding from scratch.

Why it matters

Transparency enables scrutiny, reuse and trust

In the research and infrastructure layers (the tools, data and standards the field is built on), open source lets the work be inspected, reused and improved in the open. MACH maps what's open so it can be found and reused.

  • Open means checkable. Anyone can inspect, reuse and improve the work, and problems get found and fixed in the open.
  • Machine-readable by design. Every entry is structured JSON-LD, mapped to the right open metadata standard for its type (CodeMeta, MLDCAT-AP, Croissant, DCAT-AP), so the catalogue is queryable by software and AI agents, not just browsable by people.
  • Assessed, not just listed. Each entry carries a rule-based judgement (Adopt, Assess or Caution) and a repo-health label, derived from measured signals like maintenance, adoption and licence, with the reasoning shown, so you can tell at a glance what's safe to build on.
  • Security-aware. Code repositories carry OpenSSF Scorecard and OSV vulnerability signals, so security posture is visible alongside everything else, not hidden.
  • Connected. You can see how the entries connect across the catalogue.

Further reading Malviya-Thakur et al., "Scientific Open-Source Software Is Less Likely to Become Abandoned Than One Might Think!", FSE 2025. arxiv.org/abs/2504.18971
